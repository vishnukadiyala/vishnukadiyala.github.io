\section{Research Interests}
Coordination in multi-agent systems: measuring when and with whom agents need to coordinate, learning it from data,
and acting on it. Multi-agent reinforcement learning under partial observability, learned communication between
connected vehicles, and autonomous driving.

\section{Research Experience}
\entry{Graduate Research Assistant, Multi-Agent Learning, University of Oklahoma}{Jan 2025 to present}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Coordination as a learned quantity (current, paper in preparation for ICML 2027): defined when and with
    whom an agent needs to coordinate as contrasts of one interventional value function; built a procedural
    generator of multi-agent worlds with exact ground truth and trained a transformer that, from a small budget of
    ``what if'' trials and without being told the other agents' options, ranks the agents that matter, chooses a
    plan under uncertainty about the others, and predicts outcomes. Compared against published relevance,
    attribution and regression methods on held-out world families.
  \item When predicting teammates helps cooperative agents: showed that an agent needing information about its
    partner does not imply it benefits from being trained to predict the partner, and identified when such training
    helps. Paper under review at ICLR 2027.
  \item Learned vehicle-to-vehicle communication: multi-agent reinforcement learning policies that decide when a
    connected vehicle should send its intent instead of leaving others to predict it, under bandwidth limits.
  \item Agent relevance in driving planners: measured which surrounding road users matter to a rule-based and a
    learned planner on NAVSIM (33,050 agents, labels from removing each agent and re-scoring the plan).
  \item Experiment practice: pre-registered predictions, hand-derived unit tests for every reported measure,
    confidence intervals over seeds, and training on a multi-machine setup (local GPUs and an RTX 5090 server).
\end{itemize}
